# Sector Valuation Bases and Screen Traps

Deferred reference for `idea-generation`. Read it whenever the candidate list crosses a sector boundary, before ranking names against each other, and when a screen metric looks unusually good.

Two things per sector: the valuation base the sector is actually priced on, and the traps that make a generic screen metric lie there. Which KPIs to track once a name becomes a position, and the warning sign that shows up first: `.agents/skills/thesis-tracker/references/sector-signals.md`.

One rule sits above the table: a single multiple applied across sectors ranks the accounting, not the businesses. Rank inside a sector on its own base, and compare across sectors only on returns against the cost of capital or on free cash flow yield with the reinvestment need stated.

## Software and subscription

- **Base**: enterprise value to gross profit or to recurring revenue with a growth and margin pairing (a rule-of-40 style pairing), free cash flow yield adjusted for stock compensation once growth matures.
- **Misleading generic**: price-to-earnings on GAAP losses, and EV/EBITDA where capitalised software makes EBITDA meaningless.
- **Traps**: cash flow flattered by deferred revenue timing or by billing terms that changed; net retention hiding weak new-logo growth; stock compensation excluded from every adjusted figure; a "profitable" quarter produced by cutting sales capacity.

## Semiconductors and hardware

- **Base**: mid-cycle earnings power with a multiple on it, or EV to sales bounded by mid-cycle margins; replacement value where capacity is the asset.
- **Misleading generic**: trailing price-to-earnings, which is lowest at the peak and highest at the trough.
- **Traps**: peak-cycle margins capitalised as normal; backlog treated as demand when cancellation terms are loose; inventory in the channel counted as sell-through; a low multiple that is a cycle signal rather than a value signal.

## Consumer and retail

- **Base**: EV/EBITDA with leases capitalised consistently, price-to-earnings on a normalised promotional environment, and store or door economics for growth formats.
- **Misleading generic**: price-to-book, which says little where the brand is the asset.
- **Traps**: comps against an unusually weak base; a calendar shift adding a selling week; margin held up by underinvestment in stores or marketing; inventory build read as growth preparation when it is unsold goods.

## Banks and lenders

- **Base**: price to tangible book paired with return on tangible equity, and price-to-earnings on a normalised credit cost.
- **Misleading generic**: EV/EBITDA, which has no meaning where funding is the business, and any enterprise-value measure at all.
- **Traps**: a low earnings multiple caused by understated credit costs; earnings carried by reserve releases; book value that has not absorbed securities marks; growth in the loan categories peers are exiting.

## Insurance

- **Base**: price to book paired with return on equity, and price-to-earnings on a normalised catastrophe load and no reserve development.
- **Misleading generic**: EV/EBITDA, and any measure that treats investment income as operating.
- **Traps**: prior-year reserve releases carrying the combined ratio; a light catastrophe year read as underwriting skill; growth in long-tail lines that will not show a problem for years.

## Healthcare, biotech and medtech

- **Base**: for commercial businesses, EV/EBITDA and price-to-earnings with pipeline value separated; for pre-revenue, a probability-adjusted value per asset with the probability sourced and stated.
- **Misleading generic**: any earnings multiple on a company whose value is a pipeline, and any pipeline value using the company's own success probability without saying so.
- **Traps**: channel stocking read as demand; gross-to-net erosion invisible in reported net revenue; a milestone described as a decision when it is a filing acceptance; cash runway shorter than the readout the thesis needs.

## Energy, mining and materials

- **Base**: EV to debt-adjusted cash flow at a stated deck price, net asset value per share at that deck, and free cash flow yield with maintenance capital separated from growth capital.
- **Misleading generic**: spot-price earnings multiples, which peak-invert like semiconductors.
- **Traps**: spot windfalls carried as run-rate; free cash flow that skipped maintenance capital; reserve life ignored in a terminal-value assumption; hedges rolling off into a different strip.

## Industrials

- **Base**: EV/EBITDA on mid-cycle margins, price-to-earnings with the aftermarket mix stated, and free cash flow conversion.
- **Misleading generic**: a peak-margin multiple, and any multiple that ignores backlog quality.
- **Traps**: backlog revenue read as current demand; price capture that is really cost pass-through; destocking mistaken for demand loss, or the reverse; decremental margin assumed symmetric with the incremental.

## Property and real estate

- **Base**: cash flow per share measures (funds from operations, and adjusted funds from operations after maintenance capital), net asset value against transaction-based cap rates, and implied cap rate against the debt cost.
- **Misleading generic**: price-to-earnings, which depreciation makes meaningless, and price-to-book at historical cost.
- **Traps**: funds from operations that ignores maintenance capital; occupancy held with concessions that do not appear in headline rent; a book cap rate no transaction supports; a maturity wall inside the horizon.

## Payments and fintech

- **Base**: EV to transaction profit or to net revenue after incentives, price-to-earnings once losses normalise.
- **Misleading generic**: EV to gross payment volume, and revenue presented gross of incentives.
- **Traps**: take rate falling as the merchant mix shifts to large accounts; interest on client balances counted as core earnings; credit losses on the newest lending cohort not yet visible; incentives rising to hold volume.

## Telecom, media and platforms

- **Base**: EV/EBITDA with capital intensity stated, free cash flow yield after network or content spend, and subscriber lifetime value against acquisition cost where the disclosure exists.
- **Misleading generic**: EBITDA multiples with no capital-intensity adjustment, which flatter the most capital-hungry names in the group.
- **Traps**: promotional cohorts that reprice in twelve months; subscriber counts including trials; content spend capitalised into a longer amortisation than the audience life; engagement declining ahead of revenue.

## Transport and airlines

- **Base**: EV/EBITDAR or EV/EBITDA with leases capitalised, and mid-cycle earnings power.
- **Misleading generic**: trailing earnings multiples across a fuel-driven cycle.
- **Traps**: fuel-driven margin read as operating improvement; a spot-rate spike carried into the run rate; capacity additions by competitors not yet in the forward yield; deferred maintenance flattering current cash flow.

## Utilities and regulated infrastructure

- **Base**: price-to-earnings against allowed return and rate-base growth, dividend yield with the payout's coverage, and premium to rate base.
- **Misleading generic**: EV/EBITDA without the regulatory compact, and any growth screen that reads capital spending as growth.
- **Traps**: rate-case outcomes assumed favourably; capital spending counted as earnings growth before it enters rate base; a yield supported by a payout the cash flow does not cover.
