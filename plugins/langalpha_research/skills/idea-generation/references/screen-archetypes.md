# Screen Archetypes and Their False Positives

Deferred reference for `idea-generation`. Read it before the first screen runs, to pick the archetypes that fit the mandate, and again at triage, to test each survivor against the way its archetype is characteristically wrong.

Every archetype carries three things: the signal that surfaces it, the false positives that look identical on a screen, and the evidence that separates the real one from the imitation. The screen expressions and thresholds live in the skill; what follows is what the screen cannot see.

## Long archetypes

### Quality compounder

- **Signal**: durable returns on capital above the cost of capital, reinvestment at similar returns, low earnings volatility through a cycle, an owner-operator or a management team with a reinvestment record.
- **False positives**: returns flattered by an asset base written down or fully depreciated; a moat that was regulatory and is being reopened; reinvestment that is really maintenance capital reclassified as growth; a compounder priced for a decade of compounding, where the entire debate is duration rather than quality.
- **Separator**: incremental return on invested capital over the last three years against the average, and the reinvestment rate that produced it.

### Growth at a reasonable price

- **Signal**: growth above the market with a multiple below what that growth normally attracts.
- **False positives**: growth bought with acquisitions and priced as organic; a multiple that is low because the estimate behind it is stale or wrong; growth in a segment that is a small share of profit; a cyclical peak reading as secular growth.
- **Separator**: organic growth stripped of acquisitions and currency, against the estimate revision trend.

### Revision inflection

- **Signal**: estimates turning up after a period of cuts, usually before price fully reflects it.
- **False positives**: a one-off currency, commodity or tax effect moving the estimate rather than the business; a single analyst driving the mean; price that has already run further than the revision; a late-cycle bounce read as a durable turn.
- **Separator**: the number of estimators moving, the line item the revision lands in, and whether the revision extends beyond the next quarter.

### Derated quality

- **Signal**: a business whose quality metrics still hold trading well below its own history.
- **False positives**: the derate is correct because the business changed (competition, a lost contract, a channel shift); a value trap where the multiple keeps compressing with the estimate; a balance sheet the market is pricing ahead of the income statement.
- **Separator**: which specific input the market marked down, and whether the reported numbers have started to confirm it.

### Self-help margin expansion

- **Signal**: a named cost or mix programme with a quantified target and a timetable.
- **False positives**: savings promised but reinvested rather than banked; a programme already fully in consensus; targets restated each year without the prior one being achieved; margin gains that are really a mix shift that reverses.
- **Separator**: the previous programme's realisation rate, and whether reported gross or operating margin has already moved in the direction claimed.

### Capital-allocation catalyst

- **Signal**: a pending decision that changes who owns the cash flow: a buyback authorisation, a dividend initiation, a disposal, a deleveraging path completing.
- **False positives**: an authorisation that is not an obligation and may go unused; buybacks that only offset dilution; a disposal at a price that leaves stranded costs; deleveraging into a maturity that reprices upward anyway.
- **Separator**: shares actually retired over the last four quarters, and the net-debt path against the stated plan.

### Sum-of-the-parts

- **Signal**: segments that would be valued differently apart, with a conglomerate discount to close.
- **False positives**: no mechanism and no timetable, so the discount persists indefinitely; parts valued on peers that are not comparable; tax leakage and stranded costs ignored; the "hidden" business is small enough that closing the gap changes little.
- **Separator**: a named catalyst that separates the parts, and the after-tax, after-cost value that actually reaches the holder.

### Post-earnings overreaction

- **Signal**: a large price move on a print where the multi-year earnings power looks unchanged.
- **False positives**: the move reflects a guidance change the model has not absorbed; the quarter revealed a structural problem rather than a timing one; the drift continues, since post-earnings moves are more often extended than reversed; the reaction was mechanical (index, positioning, a large holder exiting).
- **Separator**: the change in the two-year-forward estimate, not the reaction to the quarter.

## Short archetypes

Every short carries a dated catalyst, a borrow check and a defined loss path before it advances.

### Over-earning

- **Signal**: margins or returns well above the company's own history and the peer set, driven by conditions that fade.
- **False positives**: the shift is structural (mix, pricing power, a divested loss-maker); the peer comparison is unadjusted for a different business mix; normalisation is already in consensus and in the multiple.
- **Separator**: which line item carries the excess, and what specifically has to reverse for it to mean-revert.

### Deteriorating revisions

- **Signal**: consecutive downward estimate revisions with price not yet following.
- **False positives**: the cuts are finished and the next revision turns; the derate already exceeds the cut; a low-quality mean dominated by stale estimates.
- **Separator**: the trend in the newest estimates only, and whether the guidance floor has been reset.

### Quality trap

- **Signal**: a business the market still treats as high quality where the underlying metrics have started to slip.
- **False positives**: a temporary investment cycle depressing margins on purpose; a metric slipping for a disclosed and reversing reason; a franchise that recovers because customers have nowhere else to go.
- **Separator**: retention or share data over three or more periods, rather than a single soft quarter.

### Balance sheet and refinancing

- **Signal**: leverage plus a maturity inside the horizon and covenant headroom that is narrowing.
- **False positives**: undrawn facilities, asset sales or a supportive sponsor already lined up; covenants defined more loosely than they appear; the refinancing already priced and the equity trading as an option.
- **Separator**: the maturity ladder against liquidity sources, and the covenant definitions as filed rather than as summarised.

### Narrative excess

- **Signal**: a story-driven valuation with financial results far behind the story.
- **False positives**: the story is early rather than wrong, and the multiple compresses on delivery; the borrow is expensive and the crowd is already short; a capital raise resets the balance-sheet argument; the timing risk is unbounded while the narrative holds.
- **Separator**: the gap between the disclosed revenue trajectory and what the valuation requires, with a date attached.

### Accounting and cash conversion

- **Signal**: reported earnings diverging from cash, receivables or inventory building, aggressive capitalisation, growing non-GAAP adjustments.
- **False positives**: a business-model change that legitimately shifts working capital (a move to direct sales, a subscription transition); seasonal build that reverses; an acquisition making year-over-year comparisons meaningless.
- **Separator**: cash conversion over four quarters on a like-for-like basis, and whether the disclosure changed in the same period.
