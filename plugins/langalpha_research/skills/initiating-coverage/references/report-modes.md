# Initiation Modes

Deferred reference for `initiating-coverage`. Load it at Step 0, before Task 1, and again in Task 5 when a section's length or content is in question.

An initiation is written for one reader making one kind of decision. The mode fixes what the report must carry, what it drops, and how the argument is pitched. Pick one, write it down, and hold it through all five tasks. When the user has not said, infer from the request and disclose the inference in the delivery message.

| Mode | Reader and decision | Carries more of | Carries less of |
|---|---|---|---|
| Publishable | a broad client base seeing the name for the first time | complete company description, industry primer, full disclosure and methodology sections | speculative variant views, position-level specificity |
| Buy-side deep dive | an analyst deciding whether to take the work further | the debates, the falsifiers, the variant view, the disconfirming evidence | company history, boilerplate industry description, management bios |
| Long-only compounding | a multi-year holder | durability of returns on capital, reinvestment runway, capital allocation record, governance | near-term quarter dynamics, trading setup |
| Trade oriented | a shorter-horizon book | the catalyst path with dates, positioning, what is priced, the expression and its exit | long-run TAM, ten-year projections |
| Leveraged capital structure | an equity holder sitting behind material debt or leases | the financed-growth gate in full, maturity ladder, covenant headroom, after-financing returns | multiples that ignore the capital stack |
| Sector launch | a reader meeting the whole industry at once | the industry structure, where profit pools sit, the ranking logic across names | per-company depth beyond what the ranking needs |
| Model first | a reader who wants the numbers and the drivers | driver architecture, assumption defence, scenario mechanics, sensitivity | narrative sections, competitive storytelling |
| Refresh | a reader who already knows the name | what changed since the last look, and what it did to the estimates and the target | everything already established and unchanged |

## What the mode changes

- **Required sections.** A buy-side deep dive that spends four pages on company history has spent them on the wrong reader. A publishable initiation that skips the industry primer leaves its reader unable to follow the thesis.
- **Where the words go.** The 10,000-word floor is met in the sections the mode makes load-bearing, per `.agents/skills/initiating-coverage/assets/quality-checklist.md`.
- **Tone.** Publishable is neutral and complete. Buy-side is direct and adversarial towards its own thesis. Trade oriented is specific about dates and levels.
- **The cover.** The mode appears on the cover beside evidence confidence and underwriting status, so a reader knows which document they are holding.

## Preliminary and watchlist

Any mode can be published as **preliminary** or **watchlist** instead of a full initiation. This is not a lesser version of the work: it is the correct output when the evidence supports analysis but not underwriting, and it is chosen deliberately, not by running out of time.

- **Preliminary**: the analysis is real but one load-bearing input is unavailable, most often the capital structure, forward estimates, or a segment disclosure the valuation needs. Name the input, name the conclusion it blocks, and publish no target price.
- **Watchlist**: the analysis is complete and the answer is "not yet". Name the trigger, the level or the dated event that would change it, and what we would do then.

Both label the document on the cover and list, as a numbered set, the specific evidence required before ownership can be underwritten. Neither carries an invented target price, and neither is upgraded by softer language.
