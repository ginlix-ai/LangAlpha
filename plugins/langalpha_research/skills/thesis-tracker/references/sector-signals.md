# Sector KPIs and Early Warning Signs

Deferred reference for `thesis-tracker`. Read it when framing pillars or the KPI tracker for a name outside a sector you have already covered, and when a KPI moves and you need to know whether it leads or lags the thesis.

Three things per sector: the metrics a pillar can actually rest on, the warning sign that shows up before the reported numbers move, and the way the headline metric flatters the story. The valuation base each sector demands, and the screen traps that come with it, live in `.agents/skills/idea-generation/references/sector-overlays.md`.

## Software and subscription

- **Track**: net revenue retention, gross retention, new logo count and average deal size, current remaining performance obligation growth, billings against revenue, gross margin by delivery mode, sales and marketing efficiency (net new annual recurring revenue per sales dollar), rule-of-40 composition.
- **Leads the print**: gross retention slipping while net retention holds, since expansion inside the installed base masks churn for a year; hiring pauses in quota-carrying roles; a lengthening sales cycle mentioned once on a call.
- **Flatters**: net retention above 110% with new-logo growth near zero reads as health and is late-cycle expansion revenue; a billings quarter driven by early renewals; capitalised software rising while research and development flatlines.

## Semiconductors and hardware

- **Track**: bookings against billings, backlog and cancellation terms, lead times, inventory at the company and in the channel, utilisation, average selling price against unit volume, design wins with the revenue year they land in.
- **Leads the print**: lead times shortening while revenue is still rising; distributor inventory building; a mix shift to lower-margin nodes; a customer pre-building ahead of an allocation change.
- **Flatters**: peak-cycle gross margin carried forward as normal; backlog counted without cancellation rights; a supply-constrained quarter presented as demand strength.

## Consumer and retail

- **Track**: comparable sales split into traffic against ticket, unit volume against price, gross margin against promotional depth, inventory days and its composition, store or door count economics, loyalty membership and frequency, e-commerce contribution margin after fulfilment.
- **Leads the print**: traffic negative while ticket carries the comp; inventory growing faster than sales for two quarters; markdown reserve rising; a gift-card or loyalty deferral shift.
- **Flatters**: comps against a weak base quarter; a calendar shift adding a selling week; price-driven comps in a category losing units.

## Banks and lenders

- **Track**: net interest margin and its deposit beta, loan growth by category, non-performing loans and early-stage delinquency, reserve coverage and the provision build, charge-off rate against vintage, capital ratio headroom, deposit mix and cost, fee income durability.
- **Leads the print**: early-stage delinquency turning before charge-offs; reserve release supporting earnings; deposit costs rising faster than asset yields; growth concentrated in the categories peers are exiting.
- **Flatters**: earnings held up by reserve releases; a low earnings multiple that is really an understated credit cost; loan growth bought with price.

## Insurance

- **Track**: combined ratio split into loss and expense, price change against loss-cost trend, reserve development by accident year, retention and new business mix, catastrophe load against the plan, investment yield and duration.
- **Leads the print**: adverse development in the two most recent accident years; pricing decelerating below loss trend; a shift into lines with longer tails.
- **Flatters**: prior-year reserve releases carrying the combined ratio; a light catastrophe year read as underwriting improvement.

## Healthcare, biotech and medtech

- **Track**: for commercial products, scripts or procedure volume, net price against gross, payer coverage and prior-authorisation rates, inventory in the channel; for pipeline names, enrolment pace, trial readout windows, the regulatory milestone actually scheduled, cash runway against the next milestone.
- **Leads the print**: gross-to-net widening; enrolment slipping in a trial whose readout the thesis needs; a payer policy change ahead of the coverage decision; sales-force turnover in a launch.
- **Flatters**: channel stocking read as demand; a milestone described as a decision when it is a filing acceptance; the pipeline valued on a probability the company chose.

## Energy, mining and materials

- **Track**: realised price against the benchmark, differentials, production volume and decline rate, unit cash cost, capital intensity per unit added, reserve replacement, maintenance against growth capital expenditure, hedge book and its roll.
- **Leads the print**: unit cash cost rising while volume is flat; a hedge book rolling off into a lower strip; deferred maintenance; a reserve revision.
- **Flatters**: spot-price windfalls carried as run-rate; free cash flow that skipped maintenance capital expenditure; a cost per unit improved only by higher volume.

## Industrials

- **Track**: organic order growth and book-to-bill, backlog conversion and its margin, price against cost, aftermarket and services mix, capacity utilisation, working capital as a share of sales, decremental margin in a downturn.
- **Leads the print**: orders decelerating while revenue works through backlog; price capture narrowing; destocking in distribution.
- **Flatters**: backlog revenue read as demand today; a margin built on mix rather than productivity; a one-off aftermarket surge.

## Property and real estate

- **Track**: same-property net operating income, occupancy and leasing spreads, lease expiry schedule, tenant credit concentration, funds from operations against adjusted funds from operations, maintenance capital, debt maturity ladder and rate at refinancing, cap-rate evidence from actual transactions.
- **Leads the print**: leasing spreads turning negative on renewals; a maturity wall inside eighteen months; a tenant watch-list name growing as a share of rent.
- **Flatters**: funds from operations that ignores maintenance capital; occupancy held with concessions; a book cap rate no transaction supports.

## Payments and fintech

- **Track**: total payment volume against revenue take rate, transaction margin after network costs, active accounts and their cohort retention, credit losses where the model lends, funding cost, incentive spend as a share of revenue.
- **Leads the print**: take rate falling as large merchants renegotiate; incentives rising to hold volume; loss rates on the newest lending cohort.
- **Flatters**: volume growth from low-take-rate categories; revenue net of incentives presented gross; interest income on client balances counted as core.

## Telecom, media and platforms

- **Track**: subscriber additions split into gross additions against churn, average revenue per user and its drivers, content or network capital intensity, engagement per user, advertising pricing against impression growth, bundling effects on churn.
- **Leads the print**: gross additions falling while churn improvements hold the net number; pricing carried by fewer, larger advertisers; engagement per user declining ahead of revenue.
- **Flatters**: a promotional cohort that reprices in twelve months; subscriber counts including trials; content spend capitalised into a longer amortisation.

## Transport and airlines

- **Track**: yield or rate per unit against volume, load factor, capacity growth against demand, unit cost excluding fuel, fleet age and maintenance schedule, contract against spot mix.
- **Leads the print**: forward bookings softening at a stable yield; capacity being added by competitors on the same routes; unit cost rising ex-fuel.
- **Flatters**: fuel-driven margin read as operating improvement; a spot-rate spike carried into the run rate.
