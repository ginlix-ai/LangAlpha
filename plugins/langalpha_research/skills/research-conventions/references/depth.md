# Depth

Deferred reference for `research-conventions`. Load it when choosing how long a deliverable runs, when a draft is over its band, or when the user asks for a short version of something already built.

Depth is one word, chosen before the research starts, stated in the message that delivers the artifact. It sets how much work goes in. It says nothing about whether the work can be acted on, which is what the posture says.

## The three depths

| Depth | The job | What it contains | Band |
|---|---|---|---|
| `first pass` | answer one question well enough to decide whether the full work is worth doing | the question, the answer inside the first two sentences, the two or three figures the answer rests on with their as-of, the falsifier, and the one thing the next depth would add | 400 to 1,200 words, 1 to 3 pages, or up to 5 slides |
| `working analysis` | the standard research deliverable, complete on its own | the seven questions in `.agents/skills/research-conventions/references/judgment.md` answered somewhere, a sourced figure set, one comparison table (peers, segments or scenarios), a downside built through a named line item, and two to five falsifiable claims each with its monitoring item | 2,500 to 5,000 words, 8 to 12 pages, or 12 to 20 slides, plus a workbook when the numbers are meant to move |
| `diligence package` | underwrite a position or a transaction | everything in `working analysis`, plus a segment-level build in a live-formula workbook, the primary documents read rather than summarised (the filings and the transcript), capital structure and governance, a conflict register for every contested figure, and an appendix carrying the pulls behind the tables | 25 to 50 pages plus the workbook |

A skill that states its own band governs: `morning-note` is one page, `earnings-analysis` is 8 to 12 pages, `initiating-coverage` is 30 to 50. Take the band here when the calling skill is silent.

## Depth is not readiness

A `diligence package` built on thin evidence is a long artifact at `screen-grade`, not a decision-grade one. Length is not evidence, and fifty pages of it is the most convincing way to be wrong. Depth and posture travel together in one sentence near the top: depth says how much work is in it, posture says whether the inputs support acting on it. The posture table is in `.agents/skills/research-conventions/SKILL.md`.

## A short version is a rebuild

When the user asks for a shorter version of something already built, rebuild it at the lower depth. The answer, the figures it rests on and the falsifier survive; the supporting material goes. Truncating a long artifact keeps the setup and loses the conclusion, which is the half the reader asked for.

## Over the band, in cut order

Cut in this order, and stop as soon as the artifact is inside its band.

1. **Generic history.** The founding story, the acquisition timeline, anything that does not bear on the current earnings path.
2. **Market sizing with no financial consequence.** A market-size figure that no forecast, multiple or conclusion in the artifact ever touches.
3. **Management bios.** Background on the people, where governance is not one of the debates the artifact is arguing.
4. **Peer tables that end in no conclusion.** A comparison table the text never refers back to. Keep the two rows the argument uses.
5. **Sector-generic risks.** Risks that would read the same for any name in the sector, with no channel into this company's line items.

Past those five, cut scope rather than prose: drop a segment, a peer or a scenario and say in the delivery message what was dropped, instead of shortening every section until each is too thin to use.

Citation density does not move with depth: a `first pass` cites its three figures as hard as a diligence package cites its three hundred. How densely to cite is in `.agents/skills/research-conventions/references/evidence.md`.

The artifact is finished when it sits inside its band and every section left in either answers one of the seven questions or supports a figure that does.
